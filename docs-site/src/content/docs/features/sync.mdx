Bloom's "sync" is really two separate things: getting `.org` files onto your device in the first place (which Bloom doesn't do itself), and keeping Bloom's in-app index in step with whatever's currently on disk (which Bloom does continuously).

## The file-first model

Your `.org` files are the single source of truth, always. Bloom points at one folder on the device (chosen via the Android system folder picker, SAF) and treats everything in it as the vault. There's no proprietary format and no database you could lose data by deleting: the SQLite index (Room + FTS5) is entirely rebuildable from the files themselves.

## Getting files onto the device

Bloom does not implement its own cloud sync. The recommended approach is **Syncthing** paired with **Local Directory** mode: Syncthing keeps the folder in sync across your devices peer-to-peer (no account limits, no vendor lock-in), and Bloom just watches whatever ends up in that folder.

## What Bloom's sync engine actually does

Given a folder, Bloom:

1. Diffs file revisions (`mtime:size`) against its last known state
2. Re-reads and re-indexes anything changed
3. Removes anything that vanished from disk
4. Detects Syncthing `.sync-conflict-*` copies and flags them for [conflict resolution](/features/conflict-resolution)

This runs on:

- A **manual** trigger (the refresh icon on Notebooks)
- **App foreground/background** transitions
- A **periodic WorkManager job** (every 15+ minutes, respecting Doze)
- **Continuous polling** every 10 seconds while the app is in the foreground, if Continuous sync mode is enabled (shown as a foreground service notification while active)

## Status indicators

Notebook cards on the home screen show a pill for synced / modified / syncing state, so you can see at a glance whether Bloom's index matches disk.

## When the storage provider is unreachable

If the app that holds your vault folder (Seafile, Nextcloud, a cloud-sync folder) can't be reached, Bloom keeps showing the notebooks it last synced and puts an amber banner at the top of Notebooks, Outline and Agenda. Tap **Retry** in the banner to try again.

While the banner is up, Bloom is read only: notes open with their text visible but not editable, and a banner above the note says so. Nothing is deleted or changed, and your notebooks come back on their own once the provider answers.

If a rename, delete, create or save is refused, Bloom says why instead of "already exists". A note you were editing stays on screen with its unsaved text, and **Retry** in the banner saves it again.

## Sync log

Settings → Sync → **Sync log** shows a history of past sync runs: when they happened, what changed, and whether they succeeded, useful for diagnosing a stale index or confirming a background sync actually ran.

## Settings

Settings → Sync covers: the folder picker, a read-only **Provider** row naming the app that holds your vault (Local storage, Seafile, Nextcloud) and whether it lets Bloom rename, move and delete files, auto-sync mode (manual / periodic / continuous), the periodic interval, and the link to the sync log.
